# V14. Prediction intervals on the hottest days

[Validation summary](../REPORT.md) · pyair2stream 0.5.0, commit 8860fc3, 2026-10-06 09:42 UTC · run time 2.5 minutes

**Result: FAIL.**

Share of held-out daily measurements inside the 90% interval, pooled over the rivers: version 5: all days 90.0%, hottest 10% by prediction 84.0%, by air temperature 83.0%, summer 90.4%; version 8: all days 90.3%, hottest 10% by prediction 90.7%, by air temperature 91.3%, summer 91.9%. On the hottest days chosen by their measured temperature, the measurement lay above the 90% interval on 23.1%, 15.6% and below it on 0.1%, 1.1% of days (version 5, 8); a perfect model gives 14.5% above and 0.4% below, 21.4% above and 0.4% below on the days it would choose this way (5% each on fairly chosen days).

## Question

Limits are breached on the hottest days. In years the model was not calibrated on, do the prediction intervals hold on the days predicted to be hottest, and on the days with the hottest air, as well as they do on average?

## Method

For each Swiss river (calibration and validation files joined) and versions 5 and 8, leave-one-year-out cross-validation with the settings of V11 (DE, authors' bounds, Qmedia fixed) gives each held-out year's simulation and its fold's error model (sigma and rho of the training years). 1000 series are made from each (AR(1) error, as a FORWARD run does; parameter uncertainty not included), and for each measured day and each 7-day moving mean it is recorded whether the measurement lies inside the central 50%, 80%, 90%, 95% and 99% ranges. Shares inside are pooled over the three rivers for: all days; summer (June-August); the hottest 10% of each river's held-out days by predicted temperature; by air temperature (7-day mean air temperature for 7-day means); and by measured temperature. For the 90% interval the shares above and below it are given separately. Choosing days by their measured temperature is a biased selection: a perfect-model reference repeats every share with one simulated series in place of the measurements, which shows what a correct interval gives for each way of choosing days.

## Pass criterion

Pooled over the rivers, for each version, the shares of daily values inside the 80%, 90%, 95% ranges on the hottest 10% of days by prediction and by air temperature lie within the accepted range for real rivers (a miss rate between half and 1.5 times the stated one, as in V5). Summer, 7-day means and selection by measurement are reported, not judged.

## What this means

Version 5: on the days predicted to be hottest the 90% interval held 84.0% of the time, against 90.0% on all days; the measurement lay above it on 3.2% and below it on 12.8% of those days (5% each if right). The model's RMSE on those days was 1.02 °C, against 0.86 °C on all days, and its mean error +0.47 °C (positive: predicted too warm). The interval holds less often there than on average: the model's error on its hottest predictions is not its typical error, as V11 found for yearly peaks. Check intervals on hot days at your site.

Version 8: on the days predicted to be hottest the 90% interval held 90.7% of the time, against 90.3% on all days; the measurement lay above it on 6.0% and below it on 3.3% of those days (5% each if right). The model's RMSE on those days was 0.63 °C, against 0.66 °C on all days, and its mean error -0.07 °C (positive: predicted too warm).

Checking an interval only on the days when the measured temperature was highest (for example, the days a limit was exceeded) is a biased test: those days were chosen partly because their error was positive, so even for a correct interval the measurement lies above it more often than stated and below it less often. Here a perfect model had the 'measurement' above its 90% interval on 14%-21% of such days, instead of 5%. To check intervals on hot days, choose the days by the prediction or by the air temperature, as here.
