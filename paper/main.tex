\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\prompt{Why river temperature matters: aquatic ecology (fish thermal
limits), regulation (temperature limits, permits, compliance), and climate
change and abstraction scenarios. Where a decision rests on it, the
prediction must come with an honest range.}

\prompt{The modelling options: process-based energy-balance models (data
hungry), statistical regressions on air temperature (simple, but poor
outside the calibration range), and hybrid models. Introduce \aas{}
\citep{toffolon2015}, its lake counterpart \texttt{air2water}
\citep{piccolroaz2013}, its comparison with statistical approaches
\citep{piccolroaz2016}, and independent evaluations
\citep[e.g.][]{callahan2025}.}

\prompt{The gap this paper fills. The Fortran original
\citep{air2stream_code} calibrates and runs the model, and no more: a best
fit without uncertainty; no test on years not used for calibration; no
checks on its inputs or on unstable runs; air temperature and discharge must
be complete; a record must start on 1~January, unchecked; results are
plotted with a separate MATLAB script. Results used in regulation or in court
must be reproducible, tested, checked on data the model was not fitted to,
and carry a stated confidence.}

\prompt{Contributions, as a list (Table~\ref{tab:capabilities} summarises them):
(i) a verified port of every model version, integrator and objective, with
the Fortran's silent errors corrected (Sect.~\ref{sec:implementation});
(ii) checks that stop or flag bad inputs, and support for records with
gaps (Sect.~\ref{sec:implementation});
(ii$'$) an analysis of numerical stability: why the explicit Runge--Kutta
schemes the Fortran offers fail on the published parameters and in
scenarios, often without any visible sign, and a check that catches them
(Sect.~\ref{sec:stability});
(iii) uncertainty quantification designed for autocorrelated errors
(Sect.~\ref{sec:uq});
(iv) year-by-year cross-validation, with bias correction and conformal
margins (Sect.~\ref{sec:cv});
(v) tools for decisions: the chance a limit was exceeded, and paired
scenario comparisons (Sect.~\ref{sec:decisions});
(vi) a validation suite with stated pass criteria, whose failures are
reported (Sect.~\ref{sec:validation});
(vii) the outline of the paper.}

\section{The air2stream model}
\label{sec:model}

\prompt{A short recap for readers who do not know the model; the
original papers carry the derivation. Source:
\repo{docs/METHODS.md} §1--§9.}

\subsection{Governing equation and model versions}
The full eight-parameter model is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\mathrm{d}T_w}{\mathrm{d}t} = \frac{1}{\theta^{a_4}}
  \left[ a_1 + a_2 T_a - a_3 T_w
  + \theta \left( a_5 + a_6 \cos\!\left(2\pi\left(t - a_7\right)\right) - a_8 T_w \right) \right],
  \label{eq:a2s}
\end{equation}
with $T_w$ the water temperature, $T_a$ the air temperature, $t$ the time of
year as a fraction, and $\theta = Q/\bar{Q}$ the discharge scaled by its
mean over the calibration period (\texttt{Qmedia}).

\prompt{Explain each term in words (METHODS §5), and why $\bar{Q}$ is held at
the calibration value in prediction and scenario runs (METHODS §4).}

  \label{tab:versions}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}cllcc@{}}
    \toprule
    Version & Parameters & Right-hand side & Discharge & Seasonal term \\
    \midrule
    3 & $a_1$--$a_3$ & $a_1 + a_2T_a - a_3T_w$ & no & no \\
    4 & $a_1$--$a_4$ & $(a_1 + a_2T_a - a_3T_w)/\theta^{a_4}$ & yes & no \\
    5 & $a_1$--$a_3$, $a_6$, $a_7$ & $a_1 + a_2T_a - a_3T_w + a_6\cos(\cdot)$ & no & yes \\
    7 & all but $a_4$ & Eq.~\eqref{eq:a2s} with $\theta^{a_4}=1$ & yes & yes \\
    8 & $a_1$--$a_8$ & Eq.~\eqref{eq:a2s} & yes & yes \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\subsection{Numerical solution}
\prompt{The daily time step and the Fortran's four integrators:
Crank--Nicolson (\texttt{CRN}), RK4, RK2 and Euler; \pkg{} adds an
exponential scheme (\texttt{EXP}). Their stability is the subject of
Sect.~\ref{sec:stability}. Stability of

calibrated with (METHODS §6; the evidence is V6 and V13).}

\subsection{Objective functions and calibration}
\prompt{NSE \citep{nash1970}, RMSE and KGE \citep{gupta2009}, scored on daily,
weekly or monthly means. The warm-up year (METHODS §3). The Fortran's
optimizers: PSO \citep{kennedy1995} and Latin hypercube sampling.}

\section{Numerical stability: the B series, and why explicit schemes need extreme caution}
\label{sec:stability}

\prompt{Why this section matters. The model is a stiff linear ODE whenever the
water responds within a day, and its stiffness changes with discharge. The
Fortran offers four schemes, three of them explicit, with no stability check;
the 2015 paper calibrated with RK4. This section shows, from theory and on
the 30 published parameter sets, when the explicit schemes fail, why a
failing run can look plausible, why parameters cannot move between schemes,
and why a scheme stable on the record can fail in a scenario. Source:
\repo{examples/09\_integrator\_stability/README.md} (every number below comes
from its \texttt{run.py}), \repo{docs/METHODS.md} §6 and §15, V1, V2, V6, V13.}

\subsection{One number per day decides stability}
Every model version can be written as
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\mathrm{d}T_w}{\mathrm{d}t} = A(t) - B(t)\,T_w,
  \qquad
  B = \frac{a_3 + a_8\,\theta}{\theta^{a_4}} \quad\text{(version 8)},
  \label{eq:AB}
\end{equation}
where $B$ (per day) is the rate at which the water relaxes towards the
equilibrium $A/B$, and $1/B$ its response time.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{$A$ and $B$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:AB} for each version. In versions 3
  and 5 the stability of a scheme is settled by one number; in versions 4, 7
  and 8 it changes with discharge.}
  \label{tab:AB}
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{@{}cll@{}}
    \toprule
    Version & $A(t)$ & $B(t)$ \\
    \midrule
    3 & $a_1 + a_2T_a$ & $a_3$ \\
    4 & $(a_1 + a_2T_a)/\theta^{a_4}$ & $a_3/\theta^{a_4}$ \\
    5 & $a_1 + a_2T_a + a_6\cos(2\pi(t-a_7))$ & $a_3$ \\
    7 & $a_1 + a_2T_a + \theta(a_5 + a_6\cos(2\pi(t-a_7)))$ & $a_3 + a_8\theta$ \\
    8 & $[a_1 + a_2T_a + \theta(a_5 + a_6\cos(2\pi(t-a_7)))]/\theta^{a_4}$ & $(a_3 + a_8\theta)/\theta^{a_4}$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\prompt{Because the equation is linear in $T_w$, a difference between two
simulations is multiplied each step by an amplification factor $R$ that
depends only on $B$ and the scheme. The analysis is therefore exact, apart from
the ice floor \texttt{Tice\_cover}.}

\subsection{Stability limits from B-series}
\prompt{Explain the name clash: the \emph{B series} (the daily values of $B$)
and \emph{B-series} in numerical analysis \citep{hairer1974,hairer2006}. One
Runge--Kutta step expands as a sum over rooted trees
\citep{butcher2016}; for the test equation $y' = -By$ only the tall trees
(chains) survive, and the B-series collapses to the stability function
$R(z)$, $z = -B\Delta t$ (Table~\ref{tab:rz}). Consequences:
(i) no explicit Runge--Kutta method is stable for every $B$, because its $R$
is a polynomial;
(ii) the limits are where $\lvert R(-B)\rvert = 1$: 2 for Euler and Heun,
\fact{2.785} for RK4;
(iii) matching more terms of $\exp(z)$ (RK4) gives accuracy at small $B$, not
stability at large $B$; CRN is A-stable but not L-stable (fast components
zigzag); EXP is exact for the decay \citep{hochbruck2010,hairer1996}.
Give the Butcher tableaux in an appendix.}

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{Stability function $R(z)$, $z=-B\Delta t$, of each scheme with a
  one-day step, and the largest $B$ for which it is stable.}
  \label{tab:rz}
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lllc@{}}
    \toprule
    \texttt{integrator} & Method & $R(z)$ & Stable for \\
    \midrule
    \texttt{EUL} & explicit Euler & $1 + z$ & $B \le 2$ \\
    \texttt{RK2} & Heun & $1 + z + z^2/2$ & $B \le 2$ \\
    \texttt{RK4} & classical Runge--Kutta & $1 + z + z^2/2 + z^3/6 + z^4/24$ & $B \le 2.785$ \\
    \texttt{CRN} & Crank--Nicolson (implicit trapezoidal) & $(1 + z/2)/(1 - z/2)$ & all $B \ge 0$ \\
    \texttt{EXP} & exponential (integrating factor) & $\exp(z)$ & all $B \ge 0$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\subsection{Unstable runs that look plausible}
\prompt{The key danger. The ice floor cuts off every downward excursion, so an
unstable run need not blow up: Euler flips between 0 and \fact{25~°C} on the
Rh\^one; RK4 sits at 0~°C for five months on the Dischmabach
(Fig.~\ref{fig:failure}). A check on the output (not finite, or above
\SI{60}{\celsius}) misses them. \pkg{} therefore checks the B series before a
run: the largest growth of a difference over any stretch of days, the
product of the daily factors (Eq.~\ref{eq:growth}), known before anything is
simulated. Of the runs whose difference could grow more than 1,000 times,
\fact{46 of 47} were more than 1~°C off or diverged; where it could grow at
most 10 times, every RK2 run and every RK4 run but one (the RK4 calibration of
Sect.~\ref{sec:rk4limit}) stayed within \fact{0.34~°C}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:predicts}). For CRN the product telescopes, so a difference
never grows by more than about half the largest $B$ (\fact{2.6 times} at most
here), however long the run.}

\begin{equation}
  G_{m,n} = \prod_{j=m}^{n-1} R_j,
  \qquad
  R_j^{\mathrm{CRN}} = \frac{1 - B_j/2}{1 + B_{j+1}/2}
  \;\Rightarrow\;
  G^{\mathrm{CRN}}_{m,n} = \frac{1 - B_m/2}{1 + B_n/2}\prod_{j=m+1}^{n-1}\frac{1 - B_j/2}{1 + B_j/2}.
  \label{eq:growth}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Stable is not accurate, and a calibration learns its scheme}
\label{sec:rk4limit}
\prompt{Even where stable, Euler is \fact{0.45--0.98~°C} RMS from the
equation (V6: up to \fact{4.5~°C} on single days). The parameters absorb a
scheme's behaviour, so they belong to it: the 2015 (RK4) parameters run with
CRN differ from the published errors by up to \fact{0.32~°C} (V13); the 2016
(CRN) parameters are unstable with RK4 in \fact{8 of 15} sets (V2). A
calibration with RK4 keeps $B$ under RK4's limit, and can do more: on the
Dischmabach, version 5, $a_3 = $ \fact{2.768}, \fact{0.6\%} under the limit,
where RK4 leaves \fact{97\%} of a difference in place each day against the
equation's \fact{6\%}. With RK4 the fit is \fact{0.70~°C} RMSE; the same
equation solved exactly gives \fact{0.97~°C}, the difference lying in winter,
where RK4's slow decay holds the water above the ice floor
(Fig.~\ref{fig:rk4limit}). The calibration fitted a numerical effect. A
warning sign for users: a calibrated $B$ just under an explicit scheme's
limit.}

\subsection{Safeguards and recommendations}
\prompt{What \pkg{} does: CRN is the default; choosing an explicit scheme
prints a note; before a run it checks the share of days above the limit and
the largest growth, and stops above a growth of 100 or more than 10\% of days
above the limit; after a run it stops on non-finite or implausible output; a
FORWARD run refuses parameters calibrated with another scheme; in DE-MCMC and
prediction ensembles, unstable draws are dropped and counted. What users
should do: use CRN (EXP as a second opinion); use explicit schemes only to
reproduce the Fortran, with parameters calibrated with that scheme, on flows
like the calibration's; check the B series of a scenario's inputs before
running it. Say what the growth check cannot catch (inaccuracy of a stable
scheme; the RK4 calibration above), and why that argues for CRN rather than
a tighter check.}

\section{Uncertainty quantification}
\label{sec:uq}

\prompt{Present it as a chain: error model, then parameter uncertainty, then
prediction intervals. Sect.~\ref{sec:cv} checks the chain on held-out years.
Source: \repo{docs/METHODS.md} §12--§13 and \repo{docs/UNCERTAINTY.md}.}

\subsection{Error model and likelihood}
\prompt{Model errors persist from day to day, so treating them as independent
understates the uncertainty. Describe the AR(1) error model and the effective
number of observations $n_\mathrm{eff} = n/F$ \citep{bayley1946}, with $F$
summed over the pairs of scored days, so gaps are handled. Give the
log-likelihood (Eq.~\ref{eq:likelihood}); its maximum is the least-squares
fit. Explain why $\rho$ is estimated on a weekly scale rather than from
consecutive days (evidence: V4, V18; \citealp{mcinerney2020}). Compare the
exact AR(1) likelihood and the independent-error likelihood, and say why
neither is the default \citep{evin2014}.}

\begin{equation}
  \log L(\mathbf{a}) = -\frac{n_\mathrm{eff}}{2}\,\ln\!\frac{\mathrm{SSE}(\mathbf{a})}{n},
  \qquad
  F = 1 + \frac{2}{n}\sum_{i<j} \rho^{\lvert t_i - t_j\rvert}.
  \label{eq:likelihood}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Parameter uncertainty by DE-MCMC}
\prompt{The prior is uniform within the bounds. Sampling starts from the DE
best fit, with the affine-invariant ensemble sampler
\citep{goodman2010,foremanmackey2013}. Convergence is judged by the
autocorrelation time and split-$\hat{R}$ \citep{gelman2013}.}

\subsection{Prediction intervals}
\prompt{A FORWARD run draws parameter sets from the chain and adds AR(1) noise
\citep{box2015}, giving an ensemble of series. Intervals for days, 7-day and
30-day means, and yearly statistics come from the ensemble.}

\prompt{Each year is held out in turn \citep{stone1974,klemes1986}; the hidden

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\prompt{The integrator: published parameters belong to the scheme they
were calibrated with, and results obtained with explicit schemes (including
RK4 calibrations) should be re-examined before reuse at other flows
(Sect.~\ref{sec:stability}). Word this with care: the 2015 results are valid
as computed; the risk is in carrying them to other schemes or conditions.}

\prompt{Structural uncertainty: the prediction intervals cover the parameters
of one model version, not the choice of version. Example 04 (30\% abstraction,
Mentue) gives a summer change of \fact{+0.05~°C (+0.02 to +0.08)} with versions
7 and 8 but \fact{0.00} with version 4, whose ranges do not overlap: in
version 4 discharge changes only the response speed ($\theta^{a_4}$), never
the equilibrium, while in versions 7 and 8 it weights the terms $a_5$--$a_8$.
Version 8 reduces to version 7 here because its $a_4$ is indistinguishable
from zero (\fact{$-0.20$ to $0.22$}). Recommend reporting a scenario across
the plausible versions and justifying the one chosen by held-out fit and
coverage (\repo{examples/04\_scenario/compare\_versions.py}).}

\prompt{Low flows: in versions 7 and 8 the flow-linked share of the
equilibrium, $w = a_8\theta/(a_3 + a_8\theta)$, shrinks with the flow, so a
relative change of flow moves the equilibrium by $w(1-w)$ times the
difference between the flow-linked and air-driven temperatures: least when
flow is lowest. On the Mentue, taking 30\% of only the lowest 10\% of flows
changes the yearly highest 7-day mean by \fact{+0.03~°C}, against
\fact{+0.08~°C} for every day (\repo{examples/04\_scenario/flow\_regimes.py}).
Physically, low flows should matter more (shallow water, long residence
time); in the model that is $a_4$, which a record may not identify. State
this as a limit for drought and abstraction questions.}

\prompt{Limitations: a lumped model at one station; stationarity of the
parameters under climate change and extrapolation (V10); intervals on the
hottest days (V14); the exchangeability assumption behind the conformal
margins; dependence on the prior bounds; the local nature of the sensitivity
index.}

\prompt{Use in regulation and in court: what makes a result defensible
(stated criteria, held-out checks, recorded settings and versions, reruns
that give the same numbers), and what a report should state
(\repo{docs/PUBLISHED\_RESULTS.md}, ``What a publication should state'';
USER\_GUIDE §14).}

\prompt{Relation to the original: an independent port, not maintained or
endorsed by the original authors; the scientific credit for the model is
