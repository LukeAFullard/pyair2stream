\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\prompt{Why river temperature matters: aquatic ecology (fish thermal
limits), regulation (temperature limits, permits, compliance), and climate
change and abstraction scenarios. Where a decision rests on it, the
prediction must come with an honest range.}

\prompt{The modelling options: process-based energy-balance models (data
hungry), statistical regressions on air temperature (simple, but poor
outside the calibration range), and hybrid models. Introduce \aas{}
\citep{toffolon2015}, its lake counterpart \texttt{air2water}
\citep{piccolroaz2013}, its comparison with statistical approaches
\citep{piccolroaz2016}, and independent evaluations
\citep[e.g.][]{callahan2025}.}

\prompt{The gap this paper fills. The Fortran original
\citep{air2stream_code} calibrates and runs the model, and no more: a best
fit without uncertainty; no test on years not used for calibration; no
checks on its inputs or on unstable runs; air temperature and discharge must
be complete; a record must start on 1~January, unchecked; results are
plotted with a separate MATLAB script. Results used in regulation or in court
must be reproducible, tested, checked on data the model was not fitted to,
and carry a stated confidence.}

\prompt{Contributions, as a list (Table~\ref{tab:capabilities} summarises them):
(i) a verified port of every model version, integrator and objective, with
the Fortran's silent errors corrected (Sect.~\ref{sec:implementation});
(ii) checks that stop or flag bad inputs and unstable runs, and support for
records with gaps (Sect.~\ref{sec:implementation});
(iii) uncertainty quantification designed for autocorrelated errors
(Sect.~\ref{sec:uq});
(iv) year-by-year cross-validation, with bias correction and conformal
margins (Sect.~\ref{sec:cv});
(v) tools for decisions: the chance a limit was exceeded, and paired
scenario comparisons (Sect.~\ref{sec:decisions});
(vi) a validation suite with stated pass criteria, whose failures are
reported (Sect.~\ref{sec:validation});
(vii) the outline of the paper.}

\section{The air2stream model}
\label{sec:model}

\prompt{A short recap for readers who do not know the model; the
original papers carry the derivation. Source:
\repo{docs/METHODS.md} §1--§9.}

\subsection{Governing equation and model versions}
The full eight-parameter model is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\mathrm{d}T_w}{\mathrm{d}t} = \frac{1}{\theta^{a_4}}
  \left[ a_1 + a_2 T_a - a_3 T_w
  + \theta \left( a_5 + a_6 \cos\!\left(2\pi\left(t - a_7\right)\right) - a_8 T_w \right) \right],
  \label{eq:a2s}
\end{equation}
with $T_w$ the water temperature, $T_a$ the air temperature, $t$ the time of
year as a fraction, and $\theta = Q/\bar{Q}$ the discharge scaled by its
mean over the calibration period (\texttt{Qmedia}).

\prompt{Explain each term in words (METHODS §5), and why $\bar{Q}$ is held at
the calibration value in prediction and scenario runs (METHODS §4).}

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{Model versions: the simpler ones fix parameters at zero.}
  \label{tab:versions}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}cllcc@{}}
    \toprule
    Version & Parameters & Right-hand side & Discharge & Seasonal term \\
    \midrule
    3 & $a_1$--$a_3$ & $a_1 + a_2T_a - a_3T_w$ & no & no \\
    4 & $a_1$--$a_4$ & $(a_1 + a_2T_a - a_3T_w)/\theta^{a_4}$ & yes & no \\
    5 & $a_1$--$a_3$, $a_6$, $a_7$ & $a_1 + a_2T_a - a_3T_w + a_6\cos(\cdot)$ & no & yes \\
    7 & all but $a_4$ & Eq.~\eqref{eq:a2s} with $\theta^{a_4}=1$ & yes & yes \\
    8 & $a_1$--$a_8$ & Eq.~\eqref{eq:a2s} & yes & yes \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\subsection{Numerical solution}
\prompt{The daily time step and the Fortran's four integrators:
Crank--Nicolson (\texttt{CRN}), RK4, RK2 and Euler; \pkg{} adds an
exponential scheme (\texttt{EXP}, Sect.~\ref{sec:numerics}). Stability of
the explicit schemes, and why parameters belong to the scheme they were
calibrated with (METHODS §6; the evidence is V6 and V13).}

\subsection{Objective functions and calibration}
\prompt{NSE \citep{nash1970}, RMSE and KGE \citep{gupta2009}, scored on daily,
weekly or monthly means. The warm-up year (METHODS §3). The Fortran's
optimizers: PSO \citep{kennedy1995} and Latin hypercube sampling.}

\subsection{Numerical schemes and calibration}
\label{sec:numerics}
\prompt{The exponential (integrating-factor) scheme, stable like CRN
(METHODS §6). Differential evolution \citep{storn1997} with L-BFGS-B polishing
\citep{byrd1995} as the default optimizer, and why (V2: it fits at least as
well as the published parameters). PSO and Latin hypercube kept for
comparison with published work.}

\subsection{Checks on inputs and runs}
\label{sec:checks}
\prompt{The Fortran runs on silently. Summarise METHODS §15 as a few classes:
the inputs (missing, repeated or implausible values; negative discharge);
the run (stability of the explicit schemes, judged by the relaxation rate and
by how much an error can grow; non-finite or implausible output); and
consistency between runs (a prediction refuses a chain or calibration made
with another version, integrator, \texttt{Qmedia} or ice threshold, and warns
when discharge leaves the calibrated range). The data report
(\texttt{analyze\_timeseries}) makes the same checks before a run.}

\subsection{Data with gaps, other calendars and zero flow}
\label{sec:data}
\prompt{Gap-tolerant mode: the record is split into segments with complete
inputs, each started at rest (METHODS §10); the evidence that gaps do not bias
the result is V7. Records may start on any date. A 365-day calendar for
climate-model output (METHODS §2). Zero discharge with
\texttt{min\_theta\_floor} (METHODS §5).}

\section{Uncertainty quantification}
\label{sec:uq}

\prompt{Present it as a chain: error model, then parameter uncertainty, then
prediction intervals. Sect.~\ref{sec:cv} checks the chain on held-out years.
Source: \repo{docs/METHODS.md} §12--§13 and \repo{docs/UNCERTAINTY.md}.}

\subsection{Error model and likelihood}
\prompt{Model errors persist from day to day, so treating them as independent
understates the uncertainty. Describe the AR(1) error model and the effective
number of observations $n_\mathrm{eff} = n/F$ \citep{bayley1946}, with $F$
summed over the pairs of scored days, so gaps are handled. Give the
log-likelihood (Eq.~\ref{eq:likelihood}); its maximum is the least-squares
fit. Explain why $\rho$ is estimated on a weekly scale rather than from
consecutive days (evidence: V4, V18; \citealp{mcinerney2020}). Compare the
exact AR(1) likelihood and the independent-error likelihood, and say why
neither is the default \citep{evin2014}.}

\begin{equation}
  \log L(\mathbf{a}) = -\frac{n_\mathrm{eff}}{2}\,\ln\!\frac{\mathrm{SSE}(\mathbf{a})}{n},
  \qquad
  F = 1 + \frac{2}{n}\sum_{i<j} \rho^{\lvert t_i - t_j\rvert}.
  \label{eq:likelihood}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Parameter uncertainty by DE-MCMC}
\prompt{The prior is uniform within the bounds. Sampling starts from the DE
best fit, with the affine-invariant ensemble sampler
\citep{goodman2010,foremanmackey2013}. Convergence is judged by the
autocorrelation time and split-$\hat{R}$ \citep{gelman2013}.}

\subsection{Prediction intervals}
\prompt{A FORWARD run draws parameter sets from the chain and adds AR(1) noise
\citep{box2015}, giving an ensemble of series. Intervals for days, 7-day and
30-day means, and yearly statistics come from the ensemble.}

\subsection{Sensitivity analysis}
\prompt{One sentence or two: a local, one-at-a-time index (METHODS §14), and
its limits.}

\section{Checking predictions on years not used for calibration}
\label{sec:cv}

\prompt{An interval is only useful if it holds on data the model has not seen.
Source: \repo{docs/METHODS.md} §11 and \repo{docs/UNCERTAINTY.md} §8.}

\subsection{Leave-one-year-out cross-validation}
\prompt{Each year is held out in turn \citep{stone1974,klemes1986}; the hidden
year keeps its air temperature and discharge, which V12 shows does not
flatter the model \citep{burman1994}. Outputs: the error of each held-out
year, the coverage of the intervals at each level, and jackknife intervals
for the parameters \citep{kunsch1989}.}

\subsection{Correcting yearly statistics}
\prompt{The bias of each yearly statistic in the held-out years, and its
correction, in the manner of model output statistics \citep{glahn1972}
(METHODS §13; evidence V11).}

\subsection{Conformal margins}
\prompt{Optional margins widen an interval by the amount held-out years fell
outside it \citep{vovk2005,lei2018,romano2019}; state the exchangeability
caveat \citep{oliveira2024}. Evidence: V19.}
